Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning: How Machines Learn From Data

Artificial intelligence is often described as machines becoming "smart". But modern AI is based on something much more concrete: algorithms, data, mathematical models and computing power.

Artificial Intelligence Machine Learning In-depth Guide

What is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial intelligence is a broad field of computing focused on building systems that can perform tasks that normally require capabilities associated with human intelligence.

These tasks can include recognizing patterns, understanding language, making predictions, interpreting images, solving problems and generating content.

AI is therefore not one specific technology. It is an umbrella term covering many different techniques and systems.

AI and Machine Learning are not exactly the same

Artificial intelligence is the broader field. Machine learning is one of the major approaches used to build AI systems.

Traditional software is often created by explicitly writing rules that tell a computer what to do. Machine learning takes a different approach: instead of manually specifying every rule, a model can learn patterns from examples.

Simple distinction:

AI is the broader field and goal. Machine learning is a major technique used to create AI systems that learn patterns from data.

What does "learning" mean for a machine?

When people say that a machine learns, they do not mean that it develops human-like consciousness or experiences.

In machine learning, an algorithm adjusts the parameters of a mathematical model so that the model becomes better at a particular task according to a chosen objective.

The model is given examples, produces outputs and is evaluated against some target or objective. The learning process then adjusts its internal parameters.

Training Data

Data is one of the most important components of machine learning. The data provides examples from which a model can discover patterns.

For example, imagine creating a system that distinguishes between cats and dogs. The training process could use many labeled images showing which images contain cats and which contain dogs.

The quality, quantity and diversity of the training data can have a major effect on the resulting model.

Supervised Learning

In supervised learning, training examples contain known target answers or labels.

The model learns to map inputs to those targets.

For example, a system could learn to identify whether an email is spam by studying many examples that have already been labeled as spam or not spam.

Other examples include predicting house prices, recognizing objects in images and estimating the probability of a certain outcome.

Unsupervised Learning

Unsupervised learning works with data where explicit target labels are not provided in the same way.

Instead, algorithms can search for structures, relationships or groups within the data.

Clustering is one example. A system can group similar data points together without being explicitly told the correct category for every individual example.

Neural Networks

Neural networks are machine learning models made from interconnected layers of mathematical operations.

During training, their parameters are adjusted so that their outputs become increasingly useful for the task they are being trained to perform.

Deep neural networks contain many layers and have become especially important for modern AI applications such as image recognition, speech recognition and language models.

How a Neural Network Learns

A neural network receives input data and processes it through multiple layers. The network produces an output based on its current parameters.

During training, the output is compared with the desired result. The difference between them can be used to calculate how the model should adjust its parameters.

This process is repeated many times across training examples. Over time, the model can become better at the task.

Training vs Inference

Two important stages in machine learning are training and inference.

During training, the model learns its parameters from data. During inference, the trained model is used to produce predictions or outputs for new inputs.

  1. Training: The model learns patterns from examples.
  2. Inference: The trained model processes new information.

Why Models Sometimes Fail

A machine learning model does not automatically understand the real world perfectly.

If training data is incomplete, noisy, biased or unrepresentative, the model can learn undesirable patterns.

A model can also perform extremely well on familiar examples while performing poorly on situations that differ significantly from its training data.

What is Overfitting?

Overfitting occurs when a model learns the training examples too specifically instead of learning patterns that generalize well to new data.

A model that has effectively memorized characteristics of its training data may appear highly accurate during training but perform poorly when given new examples.

Machine learning systems therefore need to be evaluated using data that was not used to train the model.

Why Computing Power Matters

Modern machine learning models can contain millions, billions or even more parameters.

Training these models requires enormous numbers of mathematical operations.

GPUs and specialized AI accelerators can perform many of these operations efficiently, making large-scale machine learning possible.

AI in Everyday Life

Machine learning is already used in many systems people interact with every day.

  • Search engines
  • Recommendation systems
  • Spam detection
  • Speech recognition
  • Image recognition
  • Fraud detection
  • Translation systems
  • Generative AI

Generative AI

A major development in modern AI is generative AI. These systems can generate new text, images, audio, video and other forms of content.

Large language models are an example of generative AI systems that can produce text by learning patterns from large collections of data.

The Importance of Data Quality

A model can only learn from the information available during its training process. Poor-quality data can therefore create poor-quality results.

Data can contain errors, missing information, duplicates, biases or other problems that affect the behavior of a model.

Preparing and evaluating data is consequently an important part of building reliable machine learning systems.

Where AI is Heading

AI systems are increasingly capable of working with multiple types of information, including text, images, audio and video.

Researchers continue to work on improving reliability, efficiency, reasoning capabilities, safety and the ability of models to generalize to new situations.

The future of AI will likely involve increasingly capable systems combined with stronger evaluation, better data and more efficient computing.

Key takeaway:

Machine learning allows computers to learn patterns from data rather than relying entirely on manually written rules. Modern AI combines these learning techniques with increasingly powerful models, algorithms and computing infrastructure.
